What we’re learning,
and why it matters.

A companion to building Health Coach: the experiments we tried, the concepts behind them and the conclusions the evidence actually supports.

The thread so far. Better retrieval helps find evidence. Better tool choices can change what a model sees. Neither guarantees a supported, useful answer.

This is a learning record. Planning and acceptance remain in Linear; synthetic results do not establish private, Android or clinical usefulness.

A path through the learning

  1. Find evidence before generating answers

    Start with keyword, semantic and hybrid retrieval. See why recall is only part of the picture.

  2. Make the simple comparison fair

    A stronger keyword query changed how much benefit semantic retrieval appeared to add.

  3. Let the model choose its next move

    Watch how query formulation changes results with the same tools.

  4. Check what the answer really establishes

    Separate valid structure, supported claims, correct conclusions and owner usefulness.

Keep these distinctions close

What we observed What it does not establish
A relevant passage was retrieved The requested fact is supported
A citation resolves Every claim is cited and faithfully interpreted
An offline replay passes A model will make the same choices
A model run stops normally Its answer is useful or correct
A synthetic comparison is complete A production model or architecture has been selected

Live synthetic experiment · 2026-11-03

Finding the right passage

Can cloud search recover evidence when a question uses different words?

What we tried
76 invented workshop notes; 12 frozen questions; literal keyword, exact semantic and hybrid retrieval; six passes. No answer generation.
What happened
Semantic and hybrid found all 11 required passages at top five. Literal keyword found 2. Hybrid changed none of the semantic rankings. Both vector methods also returned unrelated neighbors for missing-evidence questions.
What it teaches
Finding something similar is different from finding support. Measure missing-evidence behavior alongside recall.
Limits and interpretation

The literal keyword baseline was weak. Small authored cases, Mac-to-cloud timing and no generated answers cannot establish private or Android usefulness.

Live synthetic experiment · 2026-11-03

A stronger baseline changes the story

Does semantic search still add value when keyword search is formulated fairly?

What we tried
Keep the original corpus and questions; add four support-boundary cases. Remove question words, preserve identifiers and OR keyword terms. Compare 16 questions over six passes.
What happened
Stronger keyword found 12/13 required passages; semantic and hybrid found 13/13. All methods found all seven held-out required passages. Semantic recovered one development paraphrase miss, while returning more irrelevant context.
What it teaches
Compare against a credible simpler method before attributing gains to added complexity.
Limits and interpretation

No automatic answers were accepted. Zero unsupported acceptance therefore measures the review policy, not a successful support classifier. The small holdout cannot establish equivalence.

Live synthetic experiment · 2026-11-03

A tool loop is not automatically a better answer

Does model-selected investigation improve on a fixed retrieval sequence?

What we tried
Five workshop questions, same model and evidence tools. Eve chose its calls; the control searched once and read every returned hit.
What happened
Real tool choices and recovery were observed, but no clear answer-quality improvement. Both approaches failed exact citation acceptance. One paraphrase case was confounded by an internal run label reaching the prompt.
What it teaches
Separate orchestration success, answer quality and citation validity. Keep execution labels outside model-visible questions.
Limits and interpretation

One pass, five authored cases and framework/context differences. No live rerun established the effect of the later label fix. No production framework was selected.

Live synthetic experiment · 2026-11-03

Same tools, different search decisions

Can model choice change investigation behavior without changing the search engine?

What we tried
Five unchanged questions per model: GPT-4.1 mini and Claude Sonnet 5.5 at low effort. Same tools, source contract and bounds; model-assisted blind rubric review.
What happened
Claude added “quiet” and found the quiet-room note on its first search. GPT missed it. All eight Claude citations resolved; GPT had nine malformed citations. One Claude answer still included an uncited factual explanation.
What it teaches
Query formulation matters. A valid citation does not guarantee complete claim coverage or faithful interpretation.
Limits and interpretation

One trial per case, different adapters and fixed model order. No deeper multi-step investigation benefit or production model choice was established.

Design record · 2026-11-03

Design the decision before the agent

How could a bounded investigation examine an apparent sleep change?

What we tried
Define four read-only tools, authored cases, evidence limits, answer validation and a strong scripted control before running models.
What happened
The design distinguishes recording-source changes, relevant owner notes, unexplained changes and no meaningful change. Both arms need two differently worded in-window searches before an unexplained conclusion.
What it teaches
Specify the evidence needed for a conclusion and the rule for preferring the simpler control before seeing results.
Limits and interpretation

A design is not runtime proof. Synthetic observation comparisons provide no diagnosis, health recommendation or demonstration of owner usefulness.

Offline software checks · 2026-11-03

What an offline replay can prove

Can the runner enforce its evidence and answer boundaries?

What we tried
Eight hand-authored replays: four cases across two arms. No provider request or credentials. Validate handles, source reads, numerical support, date windows and repair limits.
What happened
The replay exercises software contracts and the seam where a model could supply a different search query. The semantic query itself was written by a human.
What it teaches
A passing replay proves the scripted software behavior it exercises; it does not demonstrate model reasoning.
Limits and interpretation

Offline byte approximations are not provider token accounting. Structural checks do not establish that prose faithfully interprets a record.

Live synthetic experiment · 2026-11-03

Keep a negative result intact

Does the bounded agent find useful additional evidence over the control?

What we tried
First eight-run attempt exposed output-byte and tool-format problems. A separately authorized corrected eight-run attempt preserved the cases and recorded usage.
What happened
In the corrected attempt both arms reached two authored-label-correct conclusions out of four. The agent had two structurally validated answers; the control had three, including an incorrect unexplained conclusion. Neither found the differently worded travel note.
What it teaches
Agent advantage was not demonstrated. Structural validity, correct conclusions and semantic quality must be assessed separately.
Limits and interpretation

Remaining input-byte guards confounded agent repairs; rejected control drafts lacked diagnostics. Post-run fixes do not retrospectively repair results. Owner ratings remain unverified; no further paid run is authorized by the record.

Concepts to return to

Plain-language explanations, grounded in the work we have actually done.

Retrieval and RAG

Retrieval selects candidate evidence. Retrieval-augmented generation then gives that evidence to a model to help produce an answer.

Example and why it matters

In our work. A search finds the printer note; a later answer explains which ribbon it uses. The retrieval experiments stopped before answer generation.

Why it matters. Evaluate retrieval quality and answer quality separately.

Related: Embeddings and semantic search · Grounding and claim coverage

Seen in: Finding the right passage

Embeddings and semantic search

An embedding represents text numerically so related meanings can be ranked by distance. Similarity is a retrieval signal, not a truth score.

Example and why it matters

In our work. “Concentrate without people talking” can rank a note about a quiet room even without shared wording.

Why it matters. Semantic neighbors can still be irrelevant to the requested fact.

Related: Retrieval and RAG · Missing evidence and abstention

Seen in: Finding the right passage

Hybrid search and rank fusion

Hybrid search combines lexical and semantic result lists. Reciprocal-rank fusion combines rank positions rather than treating the original scores as comparable.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The tested hybrid used a fusion constant of 60. Its rankings added no required evidence over semantic search in the initial run.

Why it matters. Complexity needs a measured benefit; hybrid is an option to evaluate, not an automatic upgrade.

Related: A fair baseline · Recall, irrelevant context and precision

Seen in: Finding the right passage · A stronger baseline changes the story

A fair baseline

A baseline is a simpler credible approach used to judge whether a proposed change helps.

Example and why it matters

In our work. Removing question words and OR-ing useful terms raised original-case keyword retrieval from 2/11 to 10/11 required passages.

Why it matters. A weak comparator can make a sophisticated technique look better than its real advantage warrants.

Recall, irrelevant context and precision

Recall asks how much required evidence was found. Precision asks how much returned evidence is relevant. More context can improve coverage while adding noise.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The initial semantic top-ten results contained all 11 required passages among 100 returned passages across answerable questions. Those labels were required-passage labels, not exhaustive relevance judgments.

Why it matters. Read coverage beside irrelevant context and workload; recall alone does not measure answer quality.

Agent tool loop

A model chooses an available tool, receives the result and can choose another tool or finish. The runtime executes and bounds these choices.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The printer investigation retried a read with the full source handle, then searched specifically for a missing price.

Why it matters. Observable tool use proves a mechanism. Useful additional evidence is a separate outcome.

Related: Fixed control · Bounds and spend reservation · Query reformulation

Seen in: A tool loop is not automatically a better answer

Fixed control

A fixed control follows a predetermined evidence-gathering sequence rather than letting the model choose every step.

Example and why it matters

In our work. Search once, read all hits, then answer; or follow a predefined sleep-comparison and note-search plan.

Why it matters. A control should be strong enough that a tie supports keeping the simpler method.

Related: A fair baseline · Agent tool loop

Seen in: A tool loop is not automatically a better answer · Design the decision before the agent

Query reformulation

Rewrite a question into terms likely to appear in evidence, without changing what is being asked.

Example and why it matters

In our work. Adding “quiet” found the blue-room note with the same keyword tool. That success happened on the first search, not after a long investigation.

Why it matters. Some gains come from better tool inputs rather than a new retrieval engine.

Related: Agent tool loop · Embeddings and semantic search

Resolvable citations

A citation identifies a specific source version and evidence range that software can resolve.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The workshop contract used [document:revision:ordinal:Lstart-end]. A plausible-looking bracket with an incomplete handle failed the contract.

Why it matters. A human guessing the intended source does not establish reproducible traceability.

Related: Provenance and source freshness · Grounding and claim coverage

Provenance and source freshness

Provenance records where evidence came from. Versioning and line ranges help preserve which evidence supported a claim at that time.

Example and why it matters

In our work. A changed note needs an updated revision; deleting a note must remove its retrievable evidence.

Why it matters. Correct text with stale or inaccessible evidence is difficult to inspect and trust.

Grounding and claim coverage

Grounding asks whether the evidence actually supports what the answer says. Claim coverage asks whether each factual assertion has suitable support.

Example and why it matters

In our work. A battery citation can resolve while a separate archive explanation remains uncited. A nearby membership fee does not establish a delivery price.

Why it matters. Correct syntax does not prove faithful interpretation or complete support.

Related: Resolvable citations · Structural validation and semantic review

Seen in: Same tools, different search decisions · Keep a negative result intact

Missing evidence and abstention

When support is insufficient, explain the search boundary and what was not found. Do not turn one empty search into a claim that the entire corpus contains nothing.

Example and why it matters

In our work. “No note matched these two searches within this date window” is bounded. “Your notes contain no explanation” overstates the evidence.

Why it matters. Related neighbors and empty results can both mislead; neither settles corpus-wide absence.

Related: Grounding and claim coverage · Retrieval and RAG

Structural validation and semantic review

Deterministic checks enforce explicit contracts such as valid handles, read permissions, dates and numeric support. Semantic review asks whether the words fairly interpret the evidence.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The fixed investigation produced a structurally valid unexplained answer that missed a relevant travel note.

Why it matters. A validation pass is one gate; it is not a quality, clinical or owner-acceptance verdict.

Related: Grounding and claim coverage · Different layers of acceptance

Seen in: What an offline replay can prove · Keep a negative result intact

Offline replay versus live evidence

An offline replay uses authored responses to exercise software. A live run records actual model and provider behavior.

Example and why it matters

In our work. A replay can test a repair path without proving that a model will select a useful repair.

Why it matters. Label the kind of proof so passing software checks are not mistaken for demonstrated intelligence.

Related: The experiment harness · Different layers of acceptance

Seen in: What an offline replay can prove

The experiment harness

The harness assembles model inputs, runs tools, enforces bounds and records outputs. Its defects can distort the behavior being measured.

Example and why it matters

In our work. An internal run label confused a question. A byte guard rejected paid responses that fit the provider token limit.

Why it matters. Keep labels out of prompts, preserve safe failure diagnostics and disclose confounds rather than silently replacing results.

Related: Tokens, bytes and accounting · Offline replay versus live evidence

Frozen cases and holdouts

Fix questions and expected outcomes before running. A holdout is reserved for evaluation rather than tuning.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The retrieval-quality increment kept the original twelve questions and their development/holdout labels unchanged.

Why it matters. Repeated tuning against a tiny familiar test set can overstate generality. Authored holdouts still are not independent real-world evidence.

Related: Different layers of acceptance · A fair baseline

Different layers of acceptance

Software correctness, evidence validity, semantic quality and owner usefulness answer different questions and need different evidence.

Example and why it matters

In our work. A tool-count gate passed while exact citations failed. Later owner learning acceptance did not select a production framework or model.

Why it matters. Report the layer that passed and leave other layers explicitly unverified.

Related: Structural validation and semantic review · Frozen cases and holdouts

Seen in: Same tools, different search decisions · Keep a negative result intact

Latency and tail behavior

Measure the complete pipeline and its stages. p50 is the median; p95 describes the slower end of the observed sample.

Example and why it matters

In our work. Initial semantic warm p95 was 961.4 ms overall, while database warm p95 was 4.41 ms. Embedding work dominated this small run.

Why it matters. Optimize the measured bottleneck. Stage percentiles cannot simply be added to obtain total p95.

Related: Embeddings and semantic search · Tokens, bytes and accounting

Seen in: Finding the right passage

Tokens, bytes and accounting

Tokens are model-specific text units; bytes measure encoded data size. A byte approximation is not exact provider token accounting.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The live investigation rejected some valid-length outputs under an offline byte check. Paid boundaries were moved to provider token preflight and actual usage.

Why it matters. Use the right units and distinguish rate-derived estimates from reconciled charges.

Related: The experiment harness · Bounds and spend reservation

Seen in: What an offline replay can prove · Keep a negative result intact

Bounds and spend reservation

Bound requests, tools, evidence, time and cost. Reserve a conservative request allowance before sending; settle against usage afterward.

Example and why it matters

In our work. An unknown paid outcome retained its reservation and stopped the comparison rather than being treated as a free failure.

Why it matters. A normal model stop, a settled output limit and uncertain billing require different host behavior. Historical grants do not authorize a new run.

Exact search versus approximate indexes

Exact vector search ranks all eligible candidates. An approximate index trades recall for speed and has its own tuning and filtering behavior.

Example and why it matters

In our work. The small cloud experiment used exact cosine search. Database time was already only a few milliseconds, so it supplied no measured reason to add HNSW.

Why it matters. Benchmark the small simple path before introducing an index. See the recorded research for implementation-specific details.

Related: Embeddings and semantic search · Latency and tail behavior

Seen in: Recorded research and architecture

Observation, inference and recommendation

A recorded observation, a possible explanation and advice to act are different kinds of statement. Preserve their evidence and uncertainty boundaries.

Example and why it matters

In our work. A recording-source change can explain an apparent measurement shift without establishing a physiological change. A genetic association does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

Why it matters. The investigation work examines bounded evidence; it does not establish clinical validity or authorize treatment advice.
